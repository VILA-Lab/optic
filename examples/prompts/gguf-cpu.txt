At the start of the autumn term, a small community library plans a weekly reading program. The library serves children, parents, and older residents from several nearby neighborhoods. It has one quiet meeting room, a shelf of donated books, a modest budget, and six volunteers. Some visitors are confident readers, while others are learning the language or returning to books after many years. The volunteers want everyone to feel welcome and to leave each session with something useful to read at home. They can meet for ninety minutes on Saturday mornings, but they cannot buy many new materials or ask families to pay. Two volunteers enjoy telling stories, one has experience helping adults learn, and the others can organize books, welcome visitors, and help small groups. The librarian also wants a simple way to learn whether the program is working without making visitors fill out long forms. The first month should establish a routine that the volunteers can sustain through winter, including clear responsibilities and a sensible plan for unexpected absences. Please propose a practical plan for the first four sessions, explain how to divide the volunteers' time, and suggest a brief way to collect feedback. Keep your answer clear, concrete, and suitable for a small library with limited resources.
